\textbf{AURORA---Agentic UAV Refinement through Optimization, Reflection,
and Assurance}---is the evidence-gated Harness inside DroneDream 1.0.
Foundation models interpret intent and rank eligible methods, while
deterministic contracts authorize execution, evidence admission, holdout
isolation, and final selection. A retained
Evidence 2.8 / Prompt 1.7 online routing campaign qualifies 23/24
responses from 24 calls to the \texttt{gpt-4.1-2025-04-14} snapshot; its
single failing case is retained, and archived prompt histories are not
treated as causal comparisons. Separate contract evidence exercises
cross-Job memory and fail-closed provenance. A pinned PX4/Gazebo campaign
records 6/6 passing x500 Trials
across two seeds and nominal, steady-wind, and static-obstacle scenarios,
with RMSE 0.332254--0.444452 m and track coverage
0.949113--0.981456. Wind read-back and obstacle creation are retained for
both affected scenarios. These results establish the exercised
contracts, routes, and injections. A separate same-seed advanced-effects
subset records three passing trajectories after five requested effects
were applied and verified. Two GPS-noise boundary attempts verify six
effect injections but end at a 30.0-second PX4 readiness timeout; they
are failures, not successful GPS-noise flights. A later negative-control
Trial completes deterministic GPS denial/recovery and battery-profile
injection with all three effects verified, but fails the 25 m/s stability
gate at 31.211302 m/s; it is excluded from both pass numerators. A separate
hard-actuator probe physically stops motor 2 and verifies the joint-state
read-back, yet produces no trusted evaluation window and no successful

9/9 bundled categories have real injection/read-back evidence, while only

\section{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{1.
Introduction}}{1. Introduction}}\label{introduction}

UAV controller tuning is not a single-number search. PX4 layers rate,
attitude, velocity, and position controllers, and useful gains shift with
propulsion, mass, assembly, and scenario conditions. Its official workflow
therefore remains iterative: apply parameters, excite the system, inspect
the response, and revise. Software-in-the-loop changes the risk and cost
of that loop, not its combinatorics. A credible campaign must choose a
method and finite budget, preserve scenario identity, evaluate objectives
and constraints, distinguish parameter failure from infrastructure fault,
and reserve unseen scenarios for final selection. Derivative-free and
Bayesian methods systematize search, from reusable services to
trust-region and sparse high-dimensional variants
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_2}{\emph{2}}, \protect\hyperlink{ref_3}{\emph{3}}, \protect\hyperlink{ref_4}{\emph{4}}, \protect\hyperlink{ref_5}{\emph{5}}, \protect\hyperlink{ref_6}{\emph{6}}, \protect\hyperlink{ref_7}{\emph{7}}, \protect\hyperlink{ref_8}{\emph{8}}{]},
but still assume a correctly formed experiment. They neither translate
engineering intent into an auditable program nor prove that its declared
conditions were executed as declared in its persisted manifest or
reconciled to the retained evidence ledger.

This report therefore treats each frozen physical campaign as its own
evidence unit. The two-seed scenario matrix varies nominal, steady-wind,
and static-obstacle conditions; the later advanced-effects campaign
instead repeats one seed under a fixed five-effect profile and records
separate GPS readiness failures. A still later GPS/battery negative
control completes its trajectory and effect checks but fails stability
acceptance. A separate hard-actuator probe verifies SDF injection and
joint-state read-back but never establishes a trusted flight-evaluation
window. All four evidence units, failures, environments, and permissible
claims remain distinct. That separation allows additional Runtime evidence
to strengthen implementation coverage without converting heterogeneous
Trials into a pooled reliability estimate or optimizer comparison.
Reviewers can reproduce each denominator without inferring that a new
disturbance profile enlarges the earlier matrix or supplies missing
independent seeds for broader inference at release or erases retained
failures from any later customer-facing denominator during review.

Foundation models provide a useful interface layer, but not experimental
authority. Tool-using agent systems show that model reasoning becomes
more effective when coupled to explicit tools, machine-readable state,
feedback, reflective memory, and bounded execution interfaces
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_16}{\emph{16}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_17}{\emph{17}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_28}{\emph{28}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_29}{\emph{29}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_30}{\emph{30}}{]}. In control optimization,
however, a plausible explanation cannot prove that a scenario was
applied, a metric came from the intended trial, or a final candidate
remained isolated from holdout leakage. Those facts must come from
deterministic software and retained evidence. AURORA (Agentic UAV
Refinement through Optimization, Reflection, and Assurance), the
evidence-gated Harness inside DroneDream 1.0, therefore uses model
intelligence to interpret the task and rank specialized methods, while
deterministic compilers, versioned contracts, simulator receipts, outcome
taxonomy, and a final freeze gate authorize every consequential
transition. The model may propose the next action; it cannot declare
that action executed or promote an unverified observation. AURORA
therefore compiles conversation into a bounded optimization program
whose claims are earned by retained evidence.
This separation is operational: a route becomes admissible only after the
same Candidate, scenario, budget, Runtime, and receipt identity survive
validation. If any identity changes, the observation remains unavailable
instead of entering memory, selection, or a reported comparison.
\looseness=-1

This report makes three contributions. First, it specifies the system
boundary that separates model reasoning, tool eligibility, simulation
execution, and evidence authority. Second, it reports reproducible
routing, guard, fallback, and synthetic integration evidence without
relabeling software contracts as physical performance. Third, it defines
the experiments still required before broader simulator-quality claims
are justified. The presentation follows the causal narrative common to
modern technical reports in agentic and embodied AI
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_15}{\emph{15}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_18}{\emph{18}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_19}{\emph{19}}{]}, while keeping DroneDream's
claims tied to its own frozen artifacts and independently auditable
release boundary for independent regeneration, external verification,
and reuse without querying mutable application state or hidden Runtime
state.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{1.1 Product
scope}}{1.1 Product scope}}\label{product-scope}

The product boundary is simulation-first. The desktop application guides
scenario design, experiment planning, execution, evidence review,
recovery, and reporting. The public website explains and distributes the
product, hosts documentation and community features, and exposes
subscription entry points. Supabase supplies identity, community
features, quota accounting, and access to the managed-model gateway.
DroneDream 1.0 keeps PX4 and Gazebo simulation local to the owned
Runtime. The boundary is
equally explicit about what is not claimed: this report does not certify
real-aircraft safety, guarantee simulation-to-reality transfer, or
authorize unbounded autonomous flight actions. A model may propose or
route work, but it may not convert an unverified result into
a completed tuning outcome. AURORA must earn that transition with evidence
under the frozen release contract.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{2. Related work and design
rationale}}{2. Related work and design rationale}}\label{related-work-and-design-rationale}

\textbf{Black-box optimization supplies the search machinery, but not
the experimental constitution.} CMA-ES provides a robust derivative-free
evolutionary strategy, while Vizier and BoTorch formalize reusable
optimization services and modern Bayesian-optimization primitives. TuRBO
addresses local search in high dimensions, and SAASBO models sparse
axis-aligned structure {[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_4}{\emph{4}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_5}{\emph{5}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_6}{\emph{6}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_7}{\emph{7}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_8}{\emph{8}}{]}. Efficient Global Optimization
established the surrogate-and-acquisition loop for expensive black-box
functions, practical Bayesian optimization showed how to carry that loop
into noisy model selection, and the field review makes the remaining
choices around priors, acquisition, constraints, and parallelism
explicit {[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_31}{\emph{31}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_32}{\emph{32}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_33}{\emph{33}}{]}. These methods motivate
DroneDream's portfolio, yet each remains conditional on a correctly
specified objective, feasible domain, trial budget, and trustworthy
observation stream. DroneDream's distinct engineering problem begins one
level above the optimizer: it must decide which method is eligible,
prove which scenario was actually applied, and prevent infrastructure
faults or holdout outcomes from entering the learning history or
contaminating any later comparison after release.

\textbf{Robotic foundation models demonstrate broad semantic and action
priors, but they solve a different authority problem.} RT-1 and RT-2
established large-scale vision-language-action learning; Open
X-Embodiment and OpenVLA expanded cross-embodiment data and open model
access; \(\pi_0\) and \(\pi_{0.5}\) explored flow-based action generation and
heterogeneous co-training; JoyAI-RA continued this line with a
multi-source autonomy foundation model
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_9}{\emph{9}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_10}{\emph{10}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_11}{\emph{11}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_12}{\emph{12}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_13}{\emph{13}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_14}{\emph{14}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_15}{\emph{15}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_20}{\emph{20}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_21}{\emph{21}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_27}{\emph{27}}{]}. DroneDream does not claim an
end-to-end flight policy or place a language model in the control loop.
It uses model reasoning only to interpret intent and rank eligible
tools; deterministic software retains authority over experiment
construction, simulator execution, evidence admission, final selection,
release, and rollback through every audited product transition and later
replay. This division is essential because semantic competence cannot
substitute for execution identity, effect read-back, or retained terminal
evidence during each independent replay, release, and publication review
by an external reviewer.

\textbf{Data scale and benchmark breadth have advanced faster than
evidence semantics.} Open X-Embodiment, BridgeData V2, DROID, and AgiBot
World expand the diversity of robot experience; RoboCasa and RoboTwin
2.0 scale simulation tasks and controlled perturbations
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_11}{\emph{11}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_22}{\emph{22}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_23}{\emph{23}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_24}{\emph{24}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_25}{\emph{25}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_26}{\emph{26}}{]}. These resources strengthen
policy training and comparative evaluation, but their success metrics do
not answer whether a local UAV tuning run applied the intended scenario,
whether a failed trial was physical or infrastructural, or whether
holdout evidence leaked back into search. DroneDream therefore treats
dataset coverage, optimizer quality, and evidence validity as independent
axes: benchmark breadth or policy scores cannot substitute for evidence
about the exact experiment that generated a reported result.
\looseness=-1

\textbf{Tool-using agents clarify why that separation matters.}
SWE-agent shows the value of an explicit agent-computer interface, and
AlphaEvolve couples model proposals to evaluators and an evolutionary
loop {[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_16}{\emph{16}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_17}{\emph{17}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_28}{\emph{28}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_29}{\emph{29}},
\protect\hyperlink{ref_30}{\emph{30}}{]}. ReAct interleaves reasoning
with environmental actions; Toolformer studies learned tool invocation;
Reflexion uses feedback as episodic memory. Together they locate
capability in interfaces, evaluators, and feedback loops. DroneDream
adopts that interface principle but never treats free-form reflection as
experimental evidence. Only structured, provenance-bound,
policy-eligible outcomes may update decision memory; failure taxonomy,
holdout isolation, and the final freeze gate prevent generated narratives
from replacing the versioned simulator receipts and outcome records that support a claim.
\looseness=-1

\section{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{3. System and
method}}{3. System and method}}\label{system-and-method}

\textbf{The architecture assigns a single authority to every
transition.} The desktop owns user intent and interaction; the FastAPI
backend and worker validate job transitions and persist bounded state;
the AURORA compiles context, filters tool eligibility, and admits only
trusted outcomes; the owned Runtime supplies pinned PX4/Gazebo
dependencies; and cloud services provide identity, community data, quota
accounting, and a managed-model gateway without exposing the platform
key to clients. Separating these roles prevents a router choice from
becoming an executable experiment until deterministic compilers,
validators, and Runtime probes agree on the same versioned contract
before launch or retry. The authority graph is reconstructible from
persisted identities: every transition names its input state, approving
contract, execution owner, and evidence output. Retry and recovery must
preserve those identities, so neither path can silently convert a proposal
into an accepted execution or an unavailable observation into a
successful, admissible outcome after retry or during later replay and
publication review.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{3.1 AURORA: the evidence-gated
Harness}}{3.1 AURORA: the evidence-gated Harness}}\label{aurora-the-evidence-gated-harness}

\textbf{AURORA is a constrained decision system around simulation.}
Context binds parameters, scenarios, history, budgets, and failures.
Capability eligibility removes incompatible tools, while a phase gate
exposes only methods suited to exploration, recovery, refinement,
diversification, or verification. The router ranks that executable
surface and the optimizer proposes Candidates. Verification forces every
reduced-fidelity strategy to fidelity 1.0 before acceptance.
Provenance-bound memory admits trusted outcomes, while the final gate
freezes holdout results and rejects incomplete artifacts. Each stage
narrows authority, so fluent output cannot bypass an executable contract
or test. The retained decision trace records the selected tool together
with every filtered alternative and its rejection basis during later
replay without changing the frozen source identity or claim scope in
audit review.

\textbf{Authority narrows monotonically across the chain.} A later stage
may restrict an earlier proposal, but it may not broaden the scenario,
budget, tool, or evidence eligibility without issuing a new bound claim.
During retry, a worker may reacquire a lease and replay the same immutable
input; a changed Runtime, scenario digest, or acceptance predicate instead
creates a new evidence identity and preserves the prior attempt. Publication
obeys the same rule: a summary may project admitted fields from retained
receipts, but it cannot repair a failed gate, infer an absent metric, or pool
denominators whose scenario and seed structures differ. Figure 1 is therefore
a claim-authority map, not merely a generic model-to-tool agent workflow for
later review, replay, and independent audit.

\item
  Desktop boundary --- entry appears only after every required launch
  check passes. Terminal UI state alone authorizes readiness and exit;

  supports later recovery, support, audit replay, and service use.
\item
  Backend boundary --- drafts, Jobs, APIs, and artifacts cross only
  through validated transitions with stable failure codes. A rejection
  retains its reason, source identity, and replay receipt; it cannot
  become persisted, dispatched, or published work. The receipt remains
  independently replayable before any later release decision at scale.
\item
  AURORA authority --- only provenance-complete, policy-eligible evidence
  enters context, routing, reflection, or memory. Holdout,
  infrastructure-fault, legacy, incomplete, and drifted records remain
  unavailable to routing, selection, and replay. No later narrative can
  reinterpret a rejected row as admissible learning evidence.
\item
  Runtime boundary --- a physical claim must bind executable effect,
  expanded source, pinned dependency commits, the accepted customer
  Runtime, a published ledger, and an independently checked receipt.
  A launched process without that chain cannot support public release or
  customer-facing release qualification or review.
\item
  Cloud boundary --- authentication, community data, quota ledger, and
  model gateway remain server-authoritative; provider secrets never enter
  desktop logs or artifacts, and each managed-model debit must retain an
  authenticated account, quota-ledger entry, provider disposition, and
  reconcilable service receipt under its frozen billing contract.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Claim-time inputs are immutable before launch.} Schema
\textbf{dronedream.trial-execution-attempt-claim/v3} and digest
\textbf{execution\_input\_sha256} bind Trial, Candidate, and Job inputs
in an immutable snapshot. Outcome Contract 2.20 authorizes the scenario matrix
and constructs \textbf{TrialContext} without rereading mutable rows.
Checks classify changed inputs as \textbf{INPUT\_EVIDENCE\_DRIFT},
coordinated rewrites as \textbf{OUTCOME\_CONTRACT\_DRIFT}, and
unauthorized fields as \textbf{SCENARIO\_CONTRACT\_DRIFT}. Completion
locks Job
before Trial; historical receipts remain verifiable, new claims use v3,
and the original projection stays available for audit replay under the
same claim boundary across concurrent execution, replay, export, and
release review or customer acceptance.

\textbf{Physical claims are concurrency-safe and fairly scheduled.}
Execution Claim Gate 1.1 combines PostgreSQL FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED, a
conditional lease update, bounded retries, and an attempt-count fence.
Concurrent workers converge on a unique physical execution and accepted
outcome for each logical Trial, with no duplicate evidence across the
exercised pool. Scheduling Gate 1.0 chooses the least-served eligible
Job and preserves FIFO within it. A paired-Job regression alternates
sequential claims, preventing
desktop-lane monopoly; hosted priority and resource-cost scheduling
remain outside the current local scheduling contract, audit replay, and
evidence envelope for hosted work without weakening per-tenant fairness
or ownership isolation at scale.

\textbf{Model feedback is case-separated.}
Prompt Schema 2.3 assigns provider-safe
\mbox{training\_case\_N} aliases in
frozen order, keeping same-type cases with different weights or numeric
configurations distinct. Each admitted case carries its weight, seed
count, metrics, and closed failure counts; raw IDs, unsupported text,
and holdout evidence stay outside the prompt. This removes
feedback-grouping ambiguity without expanding model authority or
claiming simulator
fidelity, and preserves canonical order when matched Trials arrive in
reverse. The separation is preserved in every provider request and
retained routing receipt for later independent inspection and audit
replay when cases are recomputed or revisited after release under the same
frozen prompt schema and case order.

\textbf{Reproducibility extends below the random seed.} A non-unique
eigenvector factor can rotate an identical seeded normal sample when
another BLAS kernel resolves repeated covariance eigenvalues
differently. DroneDream instead uses the unique symmetric
positive-definite covariance square root and canonical unit-vector
spelling before discrete projection. The frozen optimizer transcript
hashes executed candidates, losses, feasibility,
route, fidelity, and cohort position, while non-causal GP/CMA diagnostic
matrices remain outside that decision hash
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_1}{\emph{1}}{]}. This boundary lets supported
machines replay identical candidate sequences across machines before any
provider decision receives credit. The transcript is compared at the
executed-Candidate boundary rather than against optimizer diagnostics
that may vary by library implementation. A replay mismatch therefore
identifies a decision-relevant divergence before a provider route or
reported observation is accepted, keeping numerical implementation drift
separate from evidence drift during independent replay and release
review on a different supported machine and BLAS stack.

\textbf{Feedback is verified again at the model boundary.} Optimizers,
the closed-tool router, and direct proposer share a training-feedback
compiler. It reconstructs canonical Trial rows and checks the
content-addressed outcome against Candidate identity, generation,
parameters, and Trial evidence before exposing a score. Sibling fields
cannot override those values. Divergence is quarantined rather than
turned into a bad-parameter penalty; migration-era rows remain
explicitly
unsealed and ineligible for learning, routing memory, or final selection
{[}\protect\hyperlink{ref_1}{\emph{1}}{]}. The rule preserves
auditability across both current and migration-era experiment histories
and their later replay without retroactive reinterpretation or an
unverifiable score entering routing or model-mediated recovery across
release boundaries, including later evidence migrations, rollback reviews,
and independently recomputed campaign exports after release.

Reflection is compiled from verified outcomes rather than free-form
model self-report. Evidence 2.8 retains the bounded
\textbf{observed\_outcome} cohort summary and adds a distinct cross-Job
memory gate. Only a terminal source Job whose decision and Candidate
cohort are \textbf{verified\_complete} can materialize an experience;
retrieval requires the same authenticated user, a different source Job,
and the exact structural task family. A 90-day TTL, per-Job and global
revocation, deletion cascade, parameter-catalog binding, contract-version
binding, and source-receipt binding fail closed. Prompt 1.7 may receive at
most six anonymous closed-structure observations, excluding owner or
source IDs, timestamps, parameters, seeds, holdout values, credentials,
and error text. Decision Trace 1.4 preserves the selected surface and
labels every such observation \textbf{observational\_not\_causal}.
Incomplete, legacy, revoked, expired, or drifted cohorts remain
\textbf{unavailable}; a zero-dispatch decision remains not applicable.
The frozen evaluation passes 10/10 fixtures---two positive retrievals and
eight isolation or lifecycle negatives---with zero provider, network, or
simulator calls and zero provider-identifier leaks.
Memory may guide a later decision, but it cannot assign optimizer benefit,
provider benefit, safety improvement, or portfolio-child credit without a
separately controlled intervention.
\looseness=-1

\begin{itemize}
\item
  COMPLETED is admitted as successful optimizer evidence only when a
  usable metric and modern receipt bind the exact Candidate, Trial,
  scenario, objective direction, and execution provenance; a terminal
  label without that complete chain remains unavailable for optimizer
  learning, final selection, export, or any later performance statement
  in release.
\item
  FAILED with a trusted domain failure code is admitted as a
  non-success because it may identify an infeasible or unsafe simulated
  parameter region without being mislabeled as infrastructure failure;
  the failed attempt stays in the optimization denominator with its
  scenario and Candidate identity intact through every later aggregate
  and arm comparison.
\item
  Infrastructure faults, cancellation, malformed evidence, and
  conflicting terminal states are quarantined and cannot update search
  or routing memory under any later decision path or replay; each rejected
  row retains a stable quarantine reason and remains visible to
  operational diagnostics and independent evidence accounting without
  later relabeling.
\item
  Holdout-only outcomes remain sealed for final evaluation, never return
  to training history or provider-visible reflection, and become
  selectable only through the frozen final gate under the predeclared
  metric and admissibility policy. That separation preserves an untouched
  comparator for the registered final decision or release.
\end{itemize}

Infrastructure failures, cancellation, malformed or internally
conflicting evidence, and holdout-only outcomes are isolated from
optimizer learning and routing memory. Admission requires a terminal
state, an allowed domain failure code where applicable, a
content-addressed Candidate-to-Trial match, and complete provenance for
the executed scenario. Rejected rows retain a stable quarantine reason
for diagnosis but cannot update scores, route preferences, recovery
memory, or the next candidate proposal. This evidence boundary prevents
a simulator crash, stale callback, or evaluation-only result from being
learned as if it described the quality of a parameter set. The retained
experiment ledger makes every quarantine decision independently
inspectable without allowing the rejected observation to affect later search.
Replay also rejects a status-preserving row whose receipt schema or source
identity has drifted. Replay authority therefore follows immutable bytes,
not a reused terminal label.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{4. Experiments and
evidence}}{4. Experiments and evidence}}\label{experiments-and-evidence}

This section keeps unlike evidence units separate. Development and
policy-holdout corpora test tool eligibility; source ablations test
named guards; the fallback campaign tests recovery equivalence under a
fixed mock budget; and synthetic integration tests orchestration on a
declared non-physical objective. Every result is recomputed from
persisted records and paired with a claim boundary. PX4/Gazebo
assertions remain isolated until the signed customer Runtime passes a
provenance-bound acceptance campaign, preventing software
checks and proxy outcomes from being pooled into one headline score or
silently promoted beyond their declared evidence class in any
publication-facing claim during external review, customer acceptance, or
independent replay of the frozen release.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{4.3 AURORA source-contract
ablations}}{4.3 AURORA source-contract ablations}}\label{aurora-source-contract-ablations}

The ablation suite asks a narrow question: whether each named source
guard changes an otherwise identical deterministic decision at the
boundary it is designed to protect. For every guard, the production
contract and one intentionally weakened comparator receive the same
fixed probes, expected result, and scoring rule. Production satisfies
25/25 probes, whereas the matched weakened variants satisfy 6/25 after
their designated protection is removed. This construction demonstrates
enforceable behavior for fallback, provider trust, outcome isolation,
and tool eligibility; it is not a statistical effect estimate and does
not measure optimizer quality, simulated control performance, flight
safety, or readiness. The comparison is diagnostic because each weakened
arm changes one named admission rule while inputs and expected outputs
stay fixed. A failed probe therefore localizes source-boundary
enforcement; it estimates neither customer frequency nor optimizer benefit
in physical controller campaigns or release decisions across independently
retained seed blocks and later acceptance reviews.

\textbf{Ablation scoring is row-level before it is aggregate.} Every probe
names one expected admission decision and one source guard, and the verifier
compares the exact Boolean result before computing group totals. Partial credit
cannot hide a leaked holdout row, an untrusted provider observation, an
incompatible tool, or a missing scenario profile. The weakened arm changes
only its named guard, so a discrepancy localizes contract enforcement while
unchanged rows remain controls. This design supports a source-boundary
diagnosis, not a frequency estimate for production failures or an estimate of
physical controller benefit across broader operational distributions or
deployment settings.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{4.6 Synthetic integration
campaign}}{4.6 Synthetic integration campaign}}\label{synthetic-integration-campaign}

\emph{This verified proxy experiment is deliberately labeled
non-physical. It exercises compilation, candidate generation,
deterministic evaluation, persistence, selection, holdout accounting,
export, and evidence recomputation across ten scenario schemas. The local
adapter uses frozen seeds and coefficients; no PX4, Gazebo, vehicle
dynamics, network provider, or customer credential is involved. The
result tests orchestration and evidence flow only; it does not establish
controller quality, simulator fidelity, physical performance, deployment
safety, or simulation-to-reality transfer. Its admissible use is to verify
that case-separated inputs, failures, and terminal evidence traverse the
same frozen ledger and can be independently recomputed without a live
provider or vehicle. Any later physical claim requires a separately pinned
Runtime, physical receipts, and an independently recomputed denominator.}

Simulation-queue behavior is also an experimental-validity concern.
Execution Claim Gate 1.1 binds each authorized Trial to a
content-addressed snapshot, while Scheduling Gate 1.0 admits the
least-served eligible Job's next FIFO Trial. Regressions cover
concurrent claimants, physical-attempt accounting, retry ownership, and
alternating claims across paired Jobs. These checks do not improve the
proxy objective; they prevent reported budgets and cohort comparisons
from being distorted by duplicate execution, starvation, or
shared-Runtime queue monopolization. The ledger retains every authorized
claim and terminal disposition for independent replay after every
failure, retry, recovery, and ownership transition. Queue correctness is
assessed on the admitted denominator, not worker throughput alone. Every
retry keeps its original Job, snapshot, and physical-attempt identity, so
concurrency cannot create a second eligible observation or remove the
failed attempt that preceded recovery from the retained denominator,
evidence replay, or later aggregate value. Replay is evaluated as a state
invariant rather than a throughput target. After every claim, the Job's
next-Trial cursor, attempt ordinal, Candidate snapshot, and terminal
disposition must agree across database and worker views. A retry may
re-enter execution only from its recorded failure state; it cannot acquire
a new budget, seed, scenario, or Candidate identity. The receipt also
binds the prior failure to the recovered attempt, so a worker restart
cannot replace one authorized observation with another or drop an
unavailable cell. These invariants expose concurrency faults before any
recovered value enters selection and preserve every declared arm,
scenario, and seed in later campaign tables; throughput remains separately
reported and cannot relax claim eligibility, denominator preservation, or
final evidence admission during replay, release review, or customer
qualification under an independently accepted Runtime and frozen release
receipt.

The concurrency fixtures and the synthetic objective remain separate even
when they share one Job ledger. Scheduling evidence answers who may claim
the next Trial and whether retries conserve identity; synthetic outcomes
answer whether the persisted mock campaign recomputes under its frozen
coefficients. A valid export must therefore reproduce both the
state-transition history and the objective rows without using throughput
as an outcome proxy. This separation also keeps a recovered infrastructure
attempt from being counted as an additional optimizer observation or from
silently replacing the failure that justified recovery.
The same receipt must preserve the failed attempt, recovered attempt, and
accounting identity so later reviewers can distinguish a valid retry from
an extra Trial. It must also reconcile queue events with persisted outcome
rows before either surface is summarized in a release table or customer
review package for independent audit at the frozen source boundary.

failure thresholds, and blocked randomization schedule. Final-test

benchmark ledger must bind the Runtime image, simulator and controller

or customer-acceptance claim.

The controlled comparison should progress from a default policy through
fixed CMA-ES, a preregistered specialist, the deterministic portfolio, a
memory-disabled AURORA arm, and the complete AURORA system.

Every arm must share the same scenario
set, initialization, trial budget, and terminal accounting. Specialist

portfolio comparisons require a matched exploration budget, and the
memory ablation must retain identical routing inputs. This sequence
isolates increasingly specific system contributions without treating a
method-name difference as causal evidence or attributing it to AURORA
without a preregistered intervention, matched denominator, and
independently recomputed evidence set.

\begin{itemize}
\item
  Primary outcomes bind frozen holdout loss, feasible-campaign rate,
  baseline improvement, time-to-target, and terminal failure rate to
  every reported arm and seed block, with paired uncertainty and effect
  size computed from the retained scenario-level comparison units.
  These units govern preregistered acceptance and arm-to-arm inference at
  release.
\item
  Secondary outcomes: wall time, model tokens and cost, router latency,
  recovery success, scenario-wise regret, and artifact completeness for
  every frozen comparison arm and seed block, reported alongside
  termination cause and evidence eligibility so efficiency cannot hide a
  higher failure burden or incomplete audit surface at publication time
  or review.
\item
  Use at least five independent seeds for engineering diagnostics and
  preferably ten for report-level comparisons across every reported arm;
  freeze seed blocks before final execution and publish paired
  scenario-level uncertainty before making any superiority or
  repeatability claim across the preregistered final-test matrix and all
  baseline arms in scope.
\item
  Keep timeouts, crashes, infeasible terminations, and failed Trials in
  the denominator under their predeclared terminal categories; never
  impute a successful loss, discard a difficult seed, or replace a
  missing receipt with an optimistic outcome, and never reclassify an
  unavailable execution after completed-arm results or rankings become
  visible during audit.
\item
  Report paired uncertainty, effect sizes, sample counts, and failure
  dispositions for each arm and scenario family; never publish
  superiority from one seed, one track, a post-hoc metric, or an

\end{itemize}

The registered comparison matrix is therefore the authoritative
denominator. A release must enumerate planned and realized cells before
opening final outcomes, preserve unavailable cells with their terminal
reason, and regenerate every aggregate from that matrix. A summary that
cannot be traced back to the frozen arm, seed, scenario, and failure
registry remains exploratory and cannot enter customer qualification or
any released comparative study. Its receipt must name the registry
digest and the exact planned and realized cell inventory for audit.

Independent recomputation must begin from that inventory rather than from
a summary table. The reviewer should recover every planned cell, resolve
its terminal disposition, recompute each admissible metric, and verify
that exclusions follow the frozen policy before aggregating an arm. A
receipt that lists only successful rows cannot support a comparative
claim because it cannot distinguish an intended omission from missing
evidence, a timeout, or a post-outcome deletion. The same rule applies to
appendix tables and customer-facing exports.

The analysis receipt must also freeze the statistic implementation, units,
pairing key, interval method, multiplicity treatment, and minimum evidence
required for each claim. A reviewer should be able to rebuild the primary
table from Trial-level rows without consulting a dashboard cache or manually
transcribed spreadsheet. If a planned metric cannot be computed, its cell
remains unavailable with a declared reason; a correlated diagnostic cannot
silently replace it after outcomes are visible. Any exploratory post-hoc
analysis is labeled separately, retains the same failure denominator, and
cannot satisfy the preregistered publication gate. This keeps analysis-code
changes, missing measurements, and selective metric substitution visible as
protocol events rather than allowing them to become unrecorded researcher
degrees of freedom in the final comparison after outcomes are known.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{6. Product reliability and

The desktop readiness gate is part of the product's technical contract.
The progress bar may report completion only after every check that DroneDream
can perform at launch has reached a terminal pass state. The entry
action must not become visible while a required check is pending or
failed. After entry, runtime failures still need explicit diagnostics
because network loss, disk damage, permission changes, and external
service changes cannot be permanently prevented. The gate therefore
  certifies a timestamped launch snapshot rather than promising an
  error-free future session, and post-entry faults must remain recoverable,
  attributable, and visible without silently invalidating prior work for
  every affected run. Its receipt should preserve probe timestamps,
  dependency versions, the service endpoint, account disposition, and the
  first failing stage. Recovery verification should replay install,
  upgrade, repair, offline, and damaged-cache paths without allowing stale
  success to satisfy a fresh launch or conceal the newly failing
  dependency under the same receipted probe sequence. The receipt must
  also retain the exact recovery action and the first post-recovery probe
  so a green launcher state remains attributable to one tested
  environment rather than becoming a generic assertion of platform
  health after repair or upgrade.

\textbf{Readiness is a conjunction, not a progress animation.} The
launcher requires a fresh supported-system probe, one owned and
compatible Runtime, a healthy local service, no mandatory update, and an
account accepted by the local API. Missing, stale, mismatched,
unreachable, or still-checking states remain blocking. Only the current
gate may mark readiness complete, change the environment badge to CHECKED,
and reveal the entry action. Quantitative release evidence still needs
time-to-ready
p50/p95, false-ready rate, and a clean Windows/upgrade matrix across
fresh install, upgrade, repair, and duplicate-download paths before any
release is accepted for public distribution on a supported Windows
architecture or the entry action is exposed across the declared release
matrix.

\textbf{Exit and account operations use the same recoverability
principle.} Closing warns about an active simulation or unsaved draft,
stops work that cannot survive process termination, and preserves a
recoverable draft whenever possible without trapping the user in the
application. Managed-model usage consumes the included quota through a
server-side ledger before BYOK is required; provider receipts must later
be reconciled against that ledger. Remaining evidence therefore concerns
draft-recovery and exit success rates plus quota-receipt consistency,
not a new product capability claim, across every managed-provider
transaction, recovery path, and audit replay retained by the service
ledger, with a reconciliation receipt preserved for later independent
audit of account state and quota events.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{7.1 Threats to
validity}}{7.1 Threats to validity}}\label{threats-to-validity}

The report's evidence units answer different questions and cannot be
pooled into one score. Routing cases measure tool eligibility, source
probes exercise named guards, fallback arms measure recovery equivalence,
and synthetic scenarios measure a deterministic proxy. Substituting one
unit for another remains a construct-validity error even when every
recorded value is correct and the units remain explicitly separate in
every release.

The same implementation sometimes generates, executes, and verifies an
artifact. A comparative campaign therefore still requires independent
recomputation, immutable manifests, negative controls, and failure
injection before any optimization-quality difference is described as
causal. The physical receipt remains first-party frozen evidence rather
than third-party validation, audit, or certification under the present
campaign or any later deployment decision.

Statistical validity requires the scenario, not an optimizer Trial, to
remain the primary comparison unit. Trials in one trajectory share
initialization, history, stopping conditions, and candidate ancestry, so
treating them as independent understates uncertainty. A publication
result must freeze seed blocks, report paired scenario-wise differences,
retain terminal failures in the denominator, and present effect sizes
with intervals rather than one aggregate percentage. Hyperparameters,
method choice, and stopping rules must be fixed before the final
partition opens;
exploratory findings remain useful but cannot replace the frozen
confirmatory comparison across every arm or its reported causal
estimate for any registered arm, scenario family, customer deployment, or
release decision under review.

Operational reproducibility requires more than code and a controller
vector. A repeatable run must bind the owned Runtime image, PX4/Gazebo
commits, vehicle and world assets, controller schema, scenario manifest,
optimizer settings, seeds, environment, and evidence verifier. It must
retain cancellations, timeouts, rejected callbacks, and post-launch
changes rather than successful Trials alone. The ledger already supplies
the source commit, test totals, digests, and reproduction command; the
remaining gap is a signed customer-Runtime
campaign executable on another machine without repository-specific
state, hidden assumptions, operator interpretation, or independent
auditability during replay across machines, maintainers, and independently
provisioned environments. The rerun must reproduce the same planned and
realized registry, unavailable cells, and terminal dispositions before
any metric is compared across installations, external benchmarks, or
customer qualification reviews. The replay receipt must preserve that
registry digest and the complete unavailable-cell disposition map for
audit.

External validity is bounded by controller, vehicle, world, and
disturbance combinations actually executed. Many Trials in one x500
setup do not cover fixed-wing vehicles, broader payload geometries,
actuator saturation or evaluated recovery from hard failure, estimator
dropout, battery voltage sag, or other controller structures. A broader
benchmark therefore needs a declared scenario matrix with held-out
combinations, not a convenient collection of worlds. Every cell must
explain its distinct operating condition, mutable parameters, and
invariant feasibility constraints. Results must be aggregated within and
across vehicle families so a strong
mean cannot conceal a systematic failure in one operationally important
subgroup or declared scenario family under final evaluation before a
broader cross-platform claim is admitted or published across all
preregistered vehicle, disturbance, energy, and fault families in
external review, together with their frozen acceptance predicates,
denominators, and failure rules. These limits remain explicit in every
vehicle-family table and customer-facing release claim.

Measurement validity requires more than scalar holdout loss: retain
trajectory error, settling time, overshoot, control effort, saturation,
constraint violations, termination cause, wall-clock cost, energy use,
and units.

Each metric needs frozen units, sampling rules, preprocessing, and
direction. Feasibility remains a gate because a lower aggregate can hide
oscillation or excessive actuator demand within one feasible trajectory.

Primary metrics and
admissibility rules must be registered before optimization, while
diagnostics remain visible for failure
analysis; post-hoc metric choice cannot redefine a failed campaign as
successful after execution once final outcomes become visible or a
customer-facing ranking has already been produced or released.

The next comparative campaign prioritizes matched-budget PX4/Gazebo
optimizer arms, physical component-level AURORA ablations, failure
injection, and the readiness/exit matrix. It freezes the Runtime,
controller catalog, scenario manifests, larger seed blocks, budgets,
eligibility policy, and terminal taxonomy before any final arm runs.
Matched baselines include fixed CMA-ES, a preselected specialist, the
deterministic portfolio, and isolated AURORA variants. Signed JSON and
CSV artifacts must retain failures and preserve the physical and
statistical boundaries already exposed by the six-Trial matrix. The
ledger must also show which planned comparisons never executed and why
in the retained receipt. A
causal provider-prompt study must pair Prompt 1.5, Prompt 1.6, and Prompt
1.7 calls on identical cases, model snapshot, call order, budget, and
scoring rule; retain every raw response; and report paired category
changes with uncertainty instead of selecting the best freeze. That design
separates both current and archived routing qualifications from any future
causal prompt-lift claim. The causal estimator must use paired case-level contrasts and
retain category-specific disagreements, refusals, and parse failures in
the denominator. The released audit artifact must preserve every paired
case, call order, and response disposition for independent recomputation
across every arm without selective exclusion and every published table.
For external recomputation, the registry must enumerate the planned and
realized seed block for every arm and preserve unavailable cells. A
verifier should reproduce each table from that registry without querying
application state or choosing a favorable subset after observing outcomes
during publication preparation, customer review, or independent audit. The
registry must also bind the scoring implementation, exclusion taxonomy, and
interval procedure to a versioned digest. Independent reviewers can then
distinguish an unavailable measurement from a removed failure and can test
the registered estimator without selecting a more favorable analysis after
the matched outcomes become visible.
The same digest also binds every table label and summary heading to the
published claim inventory.

present at the source freeze; a receipt never widens the associated claim
boundary across later audits, releases, publications, customer
communications, acceptance decisions, or independently repeated
qualification reviews under the frozen source ledger, and never
authorizes an absent claim, substituted artifact, later source, or
unrecorded execution in any downstream release

For this negative control, evidence admission is intentionally asymmetric:
zero exit codes and a true Trial-success flag establish that the
orchestrated run completed and yielded reviewable artifacts, whereas the
policy outcome independently remains failed. Keeping both state vectors
prevents operational completion, verified effect application, or retained
metrics from being silently substituted for a passing stability decision
in later reports, dashboards, release receipts, or customer reviews across
independently reviewed release and qualification cycles.

\section{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{9.
Conclusion}}{9. Conclusion}}\label{conclusion}

DroneDream 1.0 positions AURORA as an evidence-gated Harness for UAV
controller optimization in simulation: model reasoning interprets intent
and coordinates eligible methods, while deterministic contracts retain
authority over inputs, execution, evidence admission, and completion.
The frozen evidence set demonstrates exercised software boundaries through
routing, holdout-policy, guard-ablation, fallback-equivalence,
claim-receipt, case-separated feedback, and a 10/10 cross-Job memory
contract evaluation. That memory result is observational and
non-causal; it establishes only the enumerated software fixtures. The
retained 23/24 online routing qualification is likewise bounded to 24
calls, one frozen model snapshot, and the exact development corpus
retained for review.

Evidence 2.9 separately demonstrates equal configured budgets and
complete plan-history accounting for three scripted/mock seed blocks. Its
zero-provider, zero-network closure is not evidence of online LLM
planning quality, optimizer benefit, simulator fidelity, or flight
performance or customer acceptance at release.

A dedicated Runtime
also records a fixed two-seed, three-scenario PX4/Gazebo matrix with 6/6
passing Trials and verified wind and obstacle injection under its fixed
campaign, environment, and retained receipts.

The separately frozen advanced-physics evidence adds three passing
same-seed trajectories with verified payload, actuator-delay, barometer,
IMU, and gust effects. Its two GPS-noise injections terminate at the
30.0-second readiness boundary and remain failed non-performance
attempts. One file-backed ownership failure and three non-machine-verified
preflights remain visible rather than being removed from the history. This
retained history constrains every later success-rate calculation across
every subsequent release, acceptance record, and audit cycle.

A later negative control separately completes deterministic GPS
denial/recovery and battery-profile injection, yet fails stability because
position speed reaches 31.211302 m/s above a 25 m/s limit. It verifies the
exercised mechanisms and read-backs, not an additional passing flight,
capability-wide qualification, or a larger campaign success rate under
any release or customer-qualification denominator or evidence class in a
signed Runtime.

A subsequent hard-actuator probe separately verifies a motor-2 SDF hard
stop and joint-state response while retaining
\texttt{trial\_success=false} because no trusted evaluation window is
established. It is mechanism evidence, not evidence of stable flight,
fault-tolerant recovery, or an additional passing Trial under this probe.

Closure v2 unifies these exact-byte sources as a navigation ledger: 9/9
bundled categories have real injection/read-back evidence, while only five
categories have all retained Trials pass their performance gate. The
readiness, policy-failure, and no-window records remain distinct rather
than being converted into a nine-category performance result.

Evidence v10 binds these lineages into the latest 39-source provenance
ledger, not a new pooled experiment. It preserves the online route as a
qualified Evidence 2.8 / Prompt 1.7 record while marking it not current for
Evidence 2.9; it also keeps the Evidence 2.9 budget closure offline and
scripted. At the v10 freeze, current-source full regression, Windows Rust,
and the report/PDF gates remained outside that software export, so its
authoritative \texttt{release\_ready=false} field prevents provenance closure
from being misreported as final product or publication readiness. The later
1,284-test backend receipt closes one external gate without mutating v10 or
supplying Windows Rust, report/PDF, frontend, desktop, customer-facing
deployment, or physical qualification independently.

None of these evidence
class establishes optimizer superiority, broad stability, collision
avoidance, signed customer-Runtime acceptance, real-aircraft safety, or
simulation-to-reality transfer. The next decisive step is therefore a
larger matched-arm campaign plus signed-Runtime repetition and independent
recomputation, without weakening AURORA's provenance and leakage
boundaries during later evidence campaigns or releases.

The current evidence closure therefore changes what future experiments can safely
assert before it changes the empirical flight result itself. Claim v3,
Outcome Contract 2.20, input/outcome/scenario drift rejection, and
cross-Job fair scheduling make concurrent attempt counts, parameter
provenance, and scenario eligibility independently auditable. The
retained four-probe failure history shows why post-fix diagnostics must
remain separate from successful Trials. A decisive external campaign must
still freeze PX4/Gazebo versions, manifests, larger seed blocks, budgets,
metrics, and baseline arms before execution; retain every timeout and
failed Trial; and recompute signed outcomes outside the application
process. This separation keeps AURORA's demonstrated software assurances
and narrow execution evidence distinct from optimization-quality claims
that only a matched comparative study can establish with preregistered
uncertainty across every retained arm, failure, and scenario family in
the final registry.

separately historical, and no row contributes to a combined pass rate or
release-readiness decision. This keeps the table auditable without implying a
shared population, denominator, qualification threshold, or release surface.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{A.7 Report publication
gate}}{A.7 Report publication gate}}\label{a.7-report-publication-gate}

The report is released from a dedicated source commit rather than from a
mutable working tree. That commit contains the LaTeX source, report-owned
figures, evidence-reference manifest, claim ledger, and validation code,
but not its own attestation. Software and website evidence remain
read-only Git objects addressed by branch, commit, path, and SHA-256, so
the report cannot silently replace an upstream artifact while preserving
its displayed label.

A clean exact-source checkout builds twice with the source commit time
exported as \texttt{SOURCE\_DATE\_EPOCH}. The two PDFs must be
byte-identical before publication. The structural audit then rejects
unlocated prose, cross-page paragraphs, invalid links, gray text, LaTeX
warnings, and overflow. Explanatory paragraphs require at least 80\%
last-line occupancy; one- to three-line list items require at least 90\%.
Longer list items are reported separately as explanatory list blocks
rather than being compressed into the short-item rule.

Visual review renders every page and checks the left-aligned brand,
rounded abstract frame, heading hierarchy, readable figure and table
labels, centered placement, unsplit tables, non-overlapping graphics,
single-column references, and page-bottom balance. Copy or layout is
revised when a gate fails; hidden text, forced last-line justification,
and filler captions are prohibited. The receipt-only attestation finally
binds the source commit, PDF digest, page images, audits, and manifest
digest without creating a self-referential source hash. Downstream website
work may consume only the final PDF and its hash, not the report source,
scripts, ledger, or upstream evidence bytes.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{A.8 Evidence-reference
semantics}}{A.8 Evidence-reference semantics}}\label{a.8-evidence-reference-semantics}

Every external evidence entry separates the tested subject from the
commit that publishes its receipt. The verifier first proves both Git
objects exist, then checks ancestry, reads the exact blob at
\texttt{commit:path}, and recomputes the file SHA-256. JSON artifacts
with internal canonical digests are normalized again after removing only
their self-digest field. A later software head may therefore retain an
older online-routing freeze as an ancestor while publishing a newer,
separate Evidence 2.9 artifact; the two protocols remain independently
addressable and cannot overwrite one another's claim boundary.

Evidence 2.8 online routing, Evidence 2.9 offline budget accounting, the
v9 software bundle, the PX4/Gazebo Runtime campaign, and website
validation each keep distinct source IDs and admissible claims. Missing
objects, hash drift, non-ancestor provenance, schema drift, or an
unexpected current-version substitution fails verification. The report
stores only those identifiers and digests. It never vendors the upstream
JSON, CSV, logs, Runtime inventory, browser receipt, or retained physical
campaign tree into its own source commit.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{A.9 Claim and figure
regeneration}}{A.9 Claim and figure regeneration}}\label{a.9-claim-and-figure-regeneration}

The claim ledger maps each publication statement to one manifest source
and a set of JSON-pointer, CSV, or exact-text assertions. It also rejects
superseded totals and labels, so an older passing receipt cannot silently
return as a current result. Positive counts are paired with negative
boundaries: zero provider access does not become provider quality,
scripted/mock execution does not become physical fidelity, and a
two-seed Runtime matrix does not become statistical generalization. The
audit checks every required body literal only after the immutable source
assertions pass.

Report-owned figure scripts apply the same separation. They read frozen
Git blobs, rebuild chart inputs deterministically, and compare the new
pixels with the checked-in report media. Current routing and archived
routing remain labeled independently; v9 software probes remain
non-physical; PX4 scenario metrics retain their two-seed denominator.
Visual review then checks label size, axis legibility, centering, and
overlap at rendered-page scale rather than treating source resolution as
proof of readable publication output.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{A.10 Failure handling and
handoff}}{A.10 Failure handling and handoff}}\label{a.10-failure-handling-and-handoff}

An upstream verification failure never authorizes a substitute from the
latest branch tip. The report first classifies whether the missing unit is
a Git object, a file digest, an internal canonical digest, a source
ancestry edge, or a declared schema field. Only the affected claim remains
unavailable; unrelated frozen evidence can still be audited under its own
source ID. Raw evidence is not regenerated on the report branch, and a
source-binding defect stays visible until the producing line publishes a
new receipted artifact.

Numeric consistency is checked at the claim level rather than by
search-and-replace across prose. Counts, durations, versions, model
snapshots, scenario denominators, units, and qualifying thresholds are
asserted against the source that authorizes them. A displayed total may
appear in the abstract, main evidence section, limitations, conclusion,
appendix, and ledger only when those occurrences preserve the same
boundary. Historical Prompt 1.5 and 1.6 results, current Prompt 1.7
routing, Evidence 2.9 budget accounting, and PX4 execution therefore
remain separately named even when they share one report page.

The final handoff exposes a stable PDF, its SHA-256, and the receipt
needed to reproduce the validation decision. The receipt records the
source commit, page count, deterministic build result, audit digests, and
full visual-review inventory. It does not grant downstream ownership of
the LaTeX, chart generators, claim ledger, or evidence manifest, and it
does not convert local validation into website deployment, customer
Runtime acceptance, independent certification, or flight approval. Any
such transition requires its own source-bound receipt and release gate.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{A.11 Layout validation
semantics}}{A.11 Layout validation semantics}}\label{a.11-layout-validation-semantics}

The geometry audit reconstructs source paragraphs from rendered word
coordinates and measures the last line against the widest line in the
same block. Headings, formulas, code, captions, tables, references, and
list markers are classified separately from explanatory prose.
Explanatory paragraphs must reach 80\%; list items of at most three
rendered lines must reach 90\%. The rule is satisfied by editing meaning
or layout, never by forcing the last line, hiding glyphs, inserting
meaningless copy, or enabling \texttt{raggedbottom}.

Page-bottom coordinates are a screening signal rather than permission to
ignore the rendered page. Keeps for headings, unsplit tables, and
non-breaking paragraphs can create legitimate structural movement, so
every page is still inspected at publication scale. Tables must remain
centered and wholly on one page; figure labels must remain legible and
non-overlapping; the bibliography remains single-column and final.
Whitespace is resolved through substantive explanation and reflow while
preserving the evidence boundary, readable type, and the natural rhythm
of the reference layout.

As a final page-bottom diagnostic, the reviewer may temporarily append a
normal one-line sentinel after the terminal body block. If the sentinel
wraps or moves to the next page, the preceding page is within one
baseline of capacity; the sentinel is then removed. If it remains on the
same page, the source needs substantive explanation or a legitimate
reflow before publication. The sentinel never enters the released PDF,
claim ledger, text extraction, or visual-review receipt. It uses the same
font, line width, and keep rules as the target page; smaller or hidden
tokens are invalid because they understate capacity and bypass visual
review.
